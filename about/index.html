I am a Computer Science student with a long background in education and learning environments, moving into the practical side of technology work.

What interests me is understanding how a system works, helping users when something goes wrong, testing changes before they reach people, documenting what was done, and finding ways to reduce repetitive work.

HR and learning technology is where that fits best, because it combines technical problem-solving with user communication and learning.

How I work

Support, troubleshoot, test, document, automate, improve — in that order, and the last one is the point.

A user issue arrives. The first job is working out what the actual problem is, which is rarely the same as how it was first described. Then reproducing it, testing a solution, and confirming it works for the person who reported it — not just in principle.

After that comes the part that is easy to skip: writing down what happened, so the next person meeting the same issue starts further along. And if it keeps happening, asking whether the process itself can be simplified, checked automatically, or made less error-prone.

I enjoy the investigative part of this work rather than seeing support as something to move beyond.

Why an education background helps here

My education career gives me an unusual advantage for a learning-technology role: I understand the user side of learning systems.

I have designed training, supported people with very different levels of technical confidence, and learned that a technically correct solution is only useful if people can understand and use it.

More than a decade in education, professional communication and content-focused roles also meant extensive documentation, adapting information for different audiences, and working across cultures and professional contexts. Those skills now show up most directly in user support, training and documentation — the parts of systems work that decide whether a tool is actually adopted.

HR systems from the user and administration side

I have used SAP SuccessFactors as an employee and end user across learning and training, personal information, development and other HR processes, and handled practical employer and payroll administration through Palkka.fi.

These are not HRIS-administrator experience, but they gave me a real appreciation of how much small system and data issues matter to the people trying to complete everyday HR tasks.
