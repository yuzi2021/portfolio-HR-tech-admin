I built GRC Ops Lab to move beyond studying GRC concepts and practise how operational decisions unfold when evidence is incomplete, conflicting or misleading.

I work through the judgement-heavy process first, then explore which repeatable steps can be automated without automating the analyst's decision.

The interface is deliberately lightweight. The important part is the separation between source evidence, deterministic processing, workflow state and analyst judgement.

I use Codex and Claude Code to accelerate implementation, testing and refactoring. I define the GRC workflow, evidence relationships, decision points and automation boundary, then review and validate the implementation.

What the lab is teaching me

A dashboard can be green while the underlying population is wrong. An approved answer can become stale when operational evidence changes. A current policy does not prove that a recurring control was actually performed.

The recurring lesson is to investigate what the evidence supports before making a compliance, risk or customer-facing claim.

Current live scenarios are the first implementations in a broader GRC learning roadmap covering risk, audit, third-party risk, access governance, incidents, privacy and AI governance.
